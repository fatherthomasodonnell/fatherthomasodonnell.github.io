Father Thomas O'Donnell

Commentary on Tobit

Tobit 1

[1–2] Tribe of Napthali ... taken into captivity Tobit is a man living in exile. Bede reminds us that this symbolizes our own spiritual exile, when we were captured by the prince of this world. Despite the Christian influences on our Western culture, do we not still find ourselves in exile today?
[3] walked in the ways of truth and righteousness ... performed many acts of charity This is a book about a man whose live exemplifies the two-fold command of love of God and neighbor. How am I supposed to live while in exile? The answer is the same for everyone.
[4–8] The whole tribe of Napthali ... deserted the house of Jerusalem ... where all the tribes should sacrifice ... All the tribes that joined in apostasy used to sacrifice to the calf Baal, and so did the house of Naphtali my forefather ... I alone went often to Jerusalem for the feasts ... The tribes have abandoned God, are in the power of a foreign/evil prince, and sacrifice to a false god. The first "half" of the Decalogue (the Ten Words or Ten Commandments) is violated: love of God. They are loving something (or someone) more than the one God, to whom our lives must be wholly directed. Tobit's heart, however, remains firmly set on the one God and so fulfills the first half of the Decalogue.
[9] I married Anna, a member of our family What matters for us is not that Tobit married within his tribe, ethnically speaking, but that while in exile, it is good to marry someone of the same mind.
[10–12] all my brethren and my relatives ate the food of the Gentiles, but I kept myself from eating it ... I remembered God with all my heart. When he was near Jerusalem, Tobit kept God at the center of his love through proper worship in the Temple. Now he does it in exile by keeping the dietary laws. The interior reality of the heart is set by his external actions. (It is unclear if Anna herself ate the food of the Gentiles. Perhaps we can speculate that Tobit's "I myself" now includes his wife, just as her "I" should now include him.)
[13] the Most High gave me favor Tobit lived under the Old Law and yet the New Law is foreshadowed here! The favor which Tobit receives from God is intimately connected to Tobit's heart. Only by God's grace can we keep God at the center of our lives. And therefore any merit we receive for our faithfulness comes only from God's gracious decision to keep us faithful. And yet we must freely cooperate with God's gift of faith working through love. Our action is not in competition with God's action.
